Daily report

An honest end-of-day scorecard.

Each task shows what you planned, what you actually did, and the gap, in green, yellow or red. Breaks are counted separately, and a session log and day remark capture how the day really went.

Past days stay one tap away, so you can look back over the week.

Who it’s for

Built for students and people who work alone, especially ADHD brains.

Distractibility, time blindness and trouble starting aren’t willpower failures. They need a different kind of structure: the next task already named, the timer always visible, breaks that start themselves, and feedback at the end of the day.

Students and exam prep

  • Plan mode to set tomorrow’s study blocks tonight
  • Every timer logs itself, so study hours are on record
  • Daily planned-vs-actual report, subject by subject

ADHD-friendly focus

  • Next-task recommendations by time, energy and focus goal
  • Always-visible compact timer that hides in the tray
  • Breaks that start on their own

Solo workers at a PC

  • Plan the day without a team or a manager
  • See where deep work really happened
  • Free, offline, and nothing to sign up for

Not a fit: teams, managers watching someone else’s day, billable hours, or anyone who needs the same plan on a phone.

How it’s different

Keeps the plan. Checks the day against it.

A Pomodoro app

times a session and forgets the plan. TimeBlocks keeps the plan and checks the day against it.

A to-do app

tells you whether something is done. TimeBlocks tells you whether it got its time.

A time tracker

explains where the hours went after the fact, if you log them. TimeBlocks writes the actuals while you focus.

FAQ

Short answers.

Is it really free?

Yes. Download it, use it. No account, no trial clock, no locked features.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. Everything is stored on your own PC — there is no cloud, no account, nothing to sign into.

Which Windows versions?

Windows 10 and Windows 11. macOS and Linux are not supported right now.

Do I need to know time blocking already?

No. If you can name a task and pick a duration, you already know enough.
